# King Prompter — Request and Comedy Content Guide

This file defines how to write content that supports player creativity. It is not a fixed final content pool.

## What a good King’s request does

A request gives every player the same clear subject while leaving room to invent the visual joke. It should be understandable immediately, drawable as one image and broad enough to produce very different answers.

Good request formula:

> familiar subject + royal/personal reason + one comic uncertainty

Good requests are:

- short and concrete;
- visually answerable;
- culturally accessible;
- suggestive rather than over-written;
- flexible across absurd, clever and simple answers; and
- safe enough that ordinary players are not forced toward sensitive material.

Avoid requests that:

- say only “make something funny”;
- already contain the full punchline;
- require factual knowledge;
- require readable text inside the generated image;
- demand a particular real person’s likeness;
- depend on current politics, tragedy or protected traits; or
- are so detailed that all prompts become the same scene.

## Curated request pool examples

### Portraits and family history

- A portrait of the King’s mysterious childhood pet
- The worst royal family portrait ever painted
- The King’s favourite relative, Aunt Hilda
- The ancestor nobody in the family discusses
- The King’s official dating portrait
- A portrait proving the King was once adorable

### Heroism and false history

- The King’s most heroic moment—which definitely happened
- The battle the King claims he won alone
- The moment the King supposedly invented fire
- The King bravely saving the kingdom from something harmless
- A memorable event missing from every history book

### Animals and creatures

- The cutest bunny in the entire kingdom
- The castle’s least qualified guard animal
- A dragon attending its first royal ceremony
- The creature secretly living beneath the throne
- The King’s new emotional-support monster

### Art and culture

- A peaceful landscape worthy of the royal gallery
- The kingdom’s idea of perfect natural beauty
- A masterpiece that makes the King look sophisticated
- The painting the King wants above his bathtub
- A tasteful mural for the castle dungeon

### Inventions and public projects

- The newest and least practical invention in the kingdom
- The castle’s exciting new transportation system
- The King’s solution to a problem nobody had
- A royal machine with one obvious design flaw
- The kingdom’s wildly over-budget tourist attraction

### Celebrations and disasters

- A medieval celebration going terribly wrong
- The royal wedding’s most memorable moment
- The King’s birthday surprise arriving too early
- The kingdom’s first and last cooking competition
- A completely safe royal parade

## Constructing the three-option early-finisher poll

- Draw exactly three distinct requests from the curated pool.
- Exclude the active Round One request and recent requests according to room history.
- Avoid three choices from the same narrow subject family.
- Keep choice lengths visually comparable.
- Each option must be viable as the shared Round Two request without additional explanation.
- Randomize display order per room, not per player, so players can discuss “the second choice” consistently.
- Persist the three choice IDs, order and final result in authoritative state.

Recommended mix:

1. character/portrait request;
2. event/history request; and
3. object/place/invention request.

## Pigeon joke construction

The pigeon jokes about the castle, King, fictional production and itself. It may lightly tease the fictional artwork after reveal. It does not attack a player.

Useful joke targets:

- suspicious historical records;
- cheap production values;
- nervous Royal Squires;
- crooked frames and unsafe castle machinery;
- the King taking undeserved credit;
- the insistence that art is organic and human-made;
- gallery repairs that make damage worse.

Avoid jokes that reveal or lead the expected player answer before submission.

## Restoration template content

Every template must provide:

- one intact King in a strong central silhouette;
- clear torn negative space around the top and sides;
- enough surviving edge clues for any patch to connect visually;
- roughly equal visual prominence for every player mask;
- a neutral medieval/Renaissance painted base that tolerates mismatched additions;
- no essential face/body part of the King inside a player-editable mask; and
- a gold stitching/seam overlay that can be applied after composition.

Template variants may show the King seated on a throne, at a banquet, posing for a formal family portrait or standing at a ceremonial balcony. The central setup should remain simple so player patches create the comedy.

## Prompt moderation

Moderation should preserve absurdity, parody and harmless dark castle comedy while blocking disallowed sexual, hateful, exploitative or targeted abusive content. A rejected prompt must receive a clear private correction opportunity while time remains; at the deadline, use a safe controlled fallback. Never publicly announce why a player’s text was rejected.
