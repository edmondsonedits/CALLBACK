# King Prompter — Complete Four-Player Playthrough

This normative example shows what an ordinary match should feel like. Names, jokes and requests are illustrative; the phase order and rules are authoritative.

## Players

- Danny
- Maya
- Theo
- Rowan

## Tutorial

The TV opens in an empty castle gallery. The pigeon explains that the King wants original paintings. Each player describes a picture to a “completely human Royal Squire.” Instructions must contain fewer than 20 words, appear beside the generated painting during voting, and earn points from other players’ votes. The overall leader wins the Crown.

## Round One

The King requests:

> **A portrait of his mysterious childhood pet.**

Danny writes:

> A fat squirrel sitting on a pile of candy wrappers and junk food.

His prompt locks and its image job starts immediately. Because other players are still writing, Danny sees the optional poll:

1. The worst royal family portrait ever painted
2. A heroic moment from the King’s completely fabricated past
3. The newest and least practical invention in the kingdom

Danny votes for option 2. Maya also chooses 2, Theo chooses 3 and Rowan does not vote before submission closes. Option 2 wins 2–1. Had all three choices received no votes, the server would have guessed with room-seeded randomness.

When every Round One prompt resolves, the TV runs a three-second countdown and starts Round Two without waiting for the first paintings.

## Round Two

The King requests the poll winner:

> **A painting of his most heroic moment—which definitely happened.**

Danny writes:

> A hand-painted masterpiece of a man defeating a lion in battle.

Round Two image jobs start as prompts lock. Once everyone resolves, Round One paintings are ready for voting.

## Round One vote

Each anonymous card contains the image and exact prompt. Danny’s own card is disabled on his phone. He votes for:

> Three raccoons in a robe pretending to be one sophisticated royal hound.

Example result:

| Player | Votes | Points |
|---|---:|---:|
| Theo | 2 | 200 |
| Danny | 1 | 100 |
| Rowan | 1 | 100 |
| Maya | 0 | 0 |

Theo’s raccoon painting enters the Crown Gallery. Round Two paintings have continued generating throughout this vote and reveal.

## Round Two vote

Danny votes for:

> The King courageously fighting one hundred skeletons while hiding behind his elderly grandmother.

Example result at 150 points per vote:

| Player | Votes | Points | Running total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Rowan | 2 | 300 | 400 |
| Danny | 1 | 150 | 250 |
| Maya | 1 | 150 | 150 |
| Theo | 0 | 0 | 200 |

Rowan’s painting enters the Crown Gallery.

## Round Three — Royal restoration

The TV reveals a ripped historical portrait. The King remains seated in the centre, but four equal outer sections are missing. Each player privately receives one section.

Danny receives the upper-left section and writes:

> A bear on a unicycle juggling golden apples.

The other example contributions are:

- Queen secretly sawing through the back of the King’s throne
- Royal chef presenting a pie containing one extremely furious goose
- Tiny wizard opening a portal directly into the castle bathroom

The server generates each patch within its own mask, clips it, and stitches all four around the unchanged King. The intentionally inconsistent portrait appears as one complete art piece with visible gold seams.

## Royal Seal vote

The TV holds on the complete collage, then outlines each anonymous section and shows its exact prompt. Danny cannot select his bear section. He awards his Royal Seal to the throne-sawing Queen.

Example result at 300 points per Royal Seal:

| Player | Section | Votes | Points | Final total |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Maya | Throne-sawing Queen | 2 | 600 | 750 |
| Danny | Juggling bear | 1 | 300 | 550 |
| Theo | Furious goose pie | 1 | 300 | 500 |
| Rowan | Bathroom portal | 0 | 0 | 400 |

Maya’s section receives the Best Restoration plaque. The entire stitched portrait—not only Maya’s section—becomes the large centrepiece of the Crown Gallery.

## Ending

Maya has the highest cumulative score and receives the Crown. The final TV shot shows the raccoon painting, the skeleton-grandmother painting and the complete restored portrait. If the highest overall score had tied, every tied player would be crowned.

## What this example proves

- Every writing round begins with a concrete shared request.
- The optional poll occupies only early finishers and never blocks play.
- Image generation overlaps later player activity.
- Prompts remain visible so the room judges idea and execution together.
- Later rounds matter more: 100, then 150, then 300 points per vote.
- Round Three creates one shared spectacle without removing individual competition.
